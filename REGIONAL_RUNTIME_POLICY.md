# Wren Regional Runtime, Lore, Encounter, and World-Motion Policy — v1

This normative policy defines how the campaign expands geography, lore, encounters, weather, factions, populations, and off-screen world motion without pre-generating an encyclopedia or relying on the DM to remember to improvise change.

## Core principle

The world may be broader than Wren's knowledge, but durable hidden detail should be created only when it becomes structurally or causally useful.

Use this flow:

`known/active region -> regional runtime profile -> event-driven procedures -> world actors/processes -> derived encounter/weather/lore consequences`

Do not treat absence of generated detail as proof that no underlying answer exists. Do not pre-generate distant detail merely because it could exist.

## Regional activation horizon

Maintain enough hidden world state not only for Wren's current location, but also for nearby or connected regions whose actors/processes can plausibly affect the known-play area within the current campaign time horizon.

A world element becomes eligible for durable activation when one or more are true:
- Wren enters, approaches, investigates, or repeatedly references its area;
- an active thread, route, faction, adventure, or clue connects it to current play;
- its actions could materially affect Wren's known area within a plausible near-term horizon;
- a published source/adventure requires its state to exist before discovery;
- elapsed time, season, ecology, trade, politics, supernatural activity, or another process can propagate consequences into an active region.

Do not simulate the entire continent. Expand the activation horizon organically.

## Latent versus active world elements

Use two broad states:

### Latent
The entity/process/place can exist in broad outline but does not yet require an independent clock or repeated simulation.

Examples: a distant polity named on a map, an unvisited village with no current causal connection, a remote monster population with no route into active play.

### Active
The entity/process can independently change consequential world state and therefore carries explicit reevaluation triggers.

As applicable, record:
- current state/location/reach;
- goals or governing process;
- resources/capabilities/pressure;
- dependencies and constraints;
- possible next actions or transition classes;
- next reevaluation time/condition;
- regions/entities it can affect;
- dependent encounter, rumor, economy, ecology, weather, or faction state that must refresh after change.

Promotion from latent to active is automatic when causal relevance crosses the activation horizon.

## World-motion causes

Do not change a region merely because a dramatic development would be convenient. Consequential off-screen change must arise from at least one established cause:
- an NPC/faction/organized actor pursuing goals;
- a creature or population ecology process;
- weather, season, tide, fire, disease, migration, resource pressure, or other environmental process;
- a magical/supernatural process with established behavior;
- a published adventure/source timeline;
- an explicit random world procedure whose plausible outcomes were defined before the roll;
- a consequence of Wren's actions or other established events.

When several outcomes remain genuinely plausible after established motives/resources/geography/constraints are applied, define the plausible outcome space first and make a genuine secret random roll. Accept valid results and persist them once consequential.

## Scheduled reevaluation

Every active world element that can change independently must have an explicit trigger rather than depending on DM recollection.

Triggers may be:
- elapsed-time cadence;
- exact date/time once a calendar exists;
- state threshold;
- proximity/reach condition;
- receipt/loss of information;
- Wren/faction action;
- weather/season transition;
- completion/interruption of another event.

When time or an event crosses a trigger, process only the affected active elements. Do not scan every world entity every turn.

After resolution, schedule the next reevaluation if the element remains active.

## Regional runtime profile

Create a regional runtime profile when a region becomes part of the activation horizon. Keep it broad enough to route procedures without prematurely fixing undiscovered lore.

As applicable include:
- terrain/environment zones;
- settlement/population density;
- travel modes and route classes;
- climate baseline and current season status;
- weather relevance and generation source;
- navigation/getting-lost relevance;
- resource/readiness relevance;
- encounter-check applicability and governing core/source projection;
- encounter ingredients/ecology/social traffic rather than necessarily a fully enumerated table;
- active world actors/processes that can modify the region;
- known/player-facing lore substrate;
- unresolved/hidden details intentionally left for later generation.

## Encounter architecture

Keep four layers distinct:

1. **Published encounter procedure projection** — when/how checks occur, encounter chance, surprise, distance, reaction, etc.
2. **Regional encounter profile** — whether the location/travel mode uses routine random checks and which terrain/population assumptions apply.
3. **Local encounter table/ingredients** — what can plausibly be encountered here.
4. **Dynamic modifiers/overrides** — factions, migrations, alarms, disasters, adventures, weather, magical changes, or recent consequences that alter current possibilities.

Random encounters are not the only source of combat. Combat may arise from keyed/planned encounters, NPC/faction consequences, adventure material, hostile reactions, pursuit, lairs, wandering creatures, or other world state.

### Derived encounter content

Encounter tables should normally reflect world state rather than define it.

If a creature/faction/population becomes established in a region, refresh the affected encounter profile/table. If it leaves, is destroyed, becomes rare, gains patrols, or changes operating area, refresh only the dependent encounter content.

Do not add a monster merely because combat has been absent for a while.

### Safe areas

A safe or settled area may have no routine monster-style random encounter checks during ordinary local activity. It can still contain social encounters, weather, keyed events, rumors, crimes, accidents, faction activity, or rare threats when world state justifies them.

When governing core rules call for wilderness checks during meaningful travel through settled/patrolled terrain, use the core encounter projection and a table prepared to reflect the settled population rather than suppressing the procedure solely because the area is safer.

## Encounter-table creation timing

Prepare a full local encounter table when:
- a governing source/adventure already provides one;
- Wren begins meaningful travel/exploration in an area where checks can occur;
- repeated use makes a stable prepared table worthwhile;
- an active world process makes encounter composition consequential.

Otherwise a compact encounter-ingredients profile is sufficient.

Once a generated entry/result materially affects play, preserve the resulting world fact. One-use/special results may be exhausted or transformed rather than endlessly rerolled.

## Weather architecture

Climate is broad durable regional state; weather is time-varying state.

Create only the climate/season assumptions needed to generate plausible weather. Do not invent a full meteorological history.

Weather becomes procedurally active when it can affect:
- sea/boat travel;
- overland movement;
- visibility/navigation/getting lost;
- exposure/survival;
- encounters;
- spell or creature behavior;
- scheduled world events;
- meaningful scene conditions.

For sea travel, prefer the verified core DMG ship-weather projection when applicable. For ordinary descriptive weather with no mechanical consequence, generate conditions consistent with the established climate/season without unnecessary source lookup.

A new weather state should schedule/define the next relevant weather reevaluation rather than being forgotten after narration.

## Lore generation timing

Lore is generated progressively.

### Campaign/region initialization
Establish only structural lore required for coherence and facts Wren would reasonably know from upbringing/travel: broad geography, local culture/economy, common institutions/peoples where needed, ordinary local history or customs only to the degree useful for play.

### Area/entity activation
When a location, faction, institution, mystery, religion, or NPC becomes relevant, generate the deeper lore needed for present causal consistency and likely interaction.

### Investigation/discovery
When Wren asks, researches, listens, explores, or examines evidence, generate/retrieve only information available through those channels and preserve its reliability category.

### Hidden causality
Before a consequential clue depends on an unresolved hidden answer, establish the minimum necessary underlying truth and persist it as Established DM Truth. Do not delay until after the clue is delivered, and do not rewrite it because Wren guessed correctly.

### Published setting/adventure activation
When an active governing source establishes lore, retrieve and honor that source. Uploaded supplements remain subject to `RULES_PROJECTION_POLICY.md` activation/precedence rules and do not silently rewrite core/campaign assumptions merely because they are available.

## Epistemic categories

Preserve distinctions among:
- Established DM Truth;
- established player-known fact;
- accurate report;
- partial truth;
- distorted tradition;
- mistaken belief;
- deliberate deception/propaganda;
- rumor/hearsay unresolved;
- suspicion/inference;
- Wren's belief/inference;
- Prepared Possibility;
- Open Question.

A false rumor is still a canonical fact that the rumor exists. It is not objective truth.

## Information reliability

Use `state/rulings/knowledge-reliability-and-rumors.md` whenever an NPC/report/rumor's truthfulness or accuracy is not already established.

Context determines the plausible reliability space; genuine hidden randomness may select among remaining plausible states. Do not decide truth/error/deception retroactively for dramatic convenience.

## Dependency propagation

World-state changes automatically invalidate only affected derived/runtime artifacts.

Examples:
- population migration -> relevant encounter ingredients/table + rumors/local reactions;
- patrol increase -> encounter table/chance assumptions + road safety perceptions;
- storm -> sea travel, visibility, encounter/weather-dependent procedures;
- trade disruption -> traffic/economy/rumor profiles;
- supernatural escalation -> only regions/effects within established reach;
- faction move -> influence/reach, encounter eligibility, rumors/clues, affected clocks.

Derived encounter/weather/lore routing is disposable. Underlying canonical world state remains authority.

## Region-entry procedure

When Wren enters or commits to travel into a region without a sufficient runtime profile:
1. load source/canonical geography already established;
2. determine whether the area crosses the activation horizon;
3. create the minimum regional runtime profile needed for foreseeable play;
4. activate/create only causally relevant local/world actors and environmental processes;
5. establish encounter ingredients and prepare a full encounter table only if checks are likely;
6. establish climate/weather routing and next weather trigger if consequential;
7. preserve undiscovered specifics as unestablished rather than filling every field;
8. route any generated hidden truth through normal checkpoint/persistence rules once it becomes durable/consequential.
