# Wren Campaign Startup Discovery Policy — v1

Purpose: give a player starting a new AD&D 2nd Edition campaign a compact set of optional prompts that help establish as much—or as little—of the character, setting, tone, and campaign framework as they want before play begins.

This policy governs **startup discovery**, not mandatory pre-generation. It should help the player express preferences without turning campaign creation into a questionnaire marathon.

## Core principle

At the start of a **new campaign**, after the rules baseline is established but before ordinary play begins, offer a compact campaign-startup review.

The player may:
- answer every prompt;
- answer only the prompts they care about;
- ask the DM to generate any unanswered area;
- leave areas intentionally open for discovery through play;
- defer decisions until a later session.

Do not require the player to pre-author the world or character. Do not force exhaustive setting design before play.

Unanswered items should be treated as **open design space**, not as missing data that blocks play.

## Change-later guarantee

At startup, explicitly remind the player that these choices are **initial preferences, not irrevocable contracts**.

The player may later revise tone, setting scope, campaign emphasis, character depth, published-module preference, or other startup preferences when doing so does not contradict already established campaign facts.

When a requested change conflicts with established canon:
- preserve facts already established in play unless the player explicitly requests a safe retcon/reconciliation;
- prefer prospective changes to future emphasis, presentation, scope, module availability, or undeveloped areas;
- make the smallest necessary canonical change;
- persist durable changes through the normal campaign checkpoint/state system.

## Presentation style

Do not present the startup review as a long form to complete.

Preferred flow:
1. ask whether the player wants a quick campaign-startup review;
2. if yes, present prompts in small thematic groups;
3. allow short natural answers such as `surprise me`, `generate that`, `keep it open`, `official setting`, `mostly wilderness`, `use modules when they fit`, or `I want to define my family but not my hometown`;
4. summarize only the resulting decisions that materially constrain campaign generation;
5. begin play once enough exists for a coherent starting situation.

The player should be able to complete the review in a few minutes if desired.

## Startup prompt groups

### 1. Campaign tone and feel

Ask what general feel the player wants, if they care to specify.

Useful dimensions include:
- grounded / heroic / high-fantasy / strange / grim / warm / adventurous;
- serious vs. humorous;
- dangerous and fragile vs. more forgiving;
- mystery/discovery vs. straightforward adventure;
- political/social intrigue vs. exploration/adventure;
- horror as rare spice vs. major campaign element;
- magic as mysterious/rare vs. common/visible.

Do not require a single genre label. Mixed answers are normal.

### 2. Campaign focus

Ask what kinds of play the player is most interested in initially.

Examples:
- wilderness/travel/exploration;
- dungeons/ruins/sites;
- towns/cities/social play;
- mysteries/investigation;
- factions/politics;
- mercenary/trade/ship/road life;
- magical discovery;
- domain/stronghold play later;
- published adventures;
- mostly original adventures;
- a natural mixture.

This is an emphasis preference, not a content quota.

### 3. Official setting or original world

Ask whether the player wants:
- an official AD&D setting;
- an original world;
- an original local region inside a broader official setting;
- no decision yet, allowing the campaign to establish local play first and resolve the wider setting later.

If the player chooses an official setting, retrieve the relevant uploaded setting sources before establishing setting-specific facts.

Do not force a global setting decision when local play can begin coherently without one.

### 4. Starting region / place in the world

Ask what kind of place the player would like to begin in, if they care.

Examples:
- village or small rural community;
- frontier;
- coastal settlement or island;
- major city;
- market town;
- wilderness outpost;
- caravan/traveling start;
- shipboard start;
- monastery/temple/school;
- military/mercenary context;
- isolated homestead;
- somewhere generated by the DM.

Optionally ask for environmental preference:
- coast;
- forest;
- mountains;
- plains;
- desert;
- swamp;
- cold north;
- tropical region;
- urban-heavy region;
- no preference.

Do not ask the player to design geography unless they want to.

### 5. How much of the world should be predefined

Ask how much they want to establish before session one:
- **minimal** — only enough for the opening situation; discover almost everything in play;
- **light** — establish hometown/region and a few important facts;
- **moderate** — establish nearby settlements, major factions, faiths/cultures, and known regional context;
- **detailed** — collaboratively establish a substantial starting region;
- **DM-generated** — player gives broad preferences and the DM builds the rest privately.

Unestablished facts remain open until play or source constraints establish them.

### 6. Character concept depth

Ask how much of the PC the player wants to define before play:
- **mechanics only** — class/race/stats/equipment and start playing;
- **light concept** — a few personality traits, origin, and motivation;
- **moderate** — family/background/relationships/training/important memories;
- **deep** — substantial history and social context;
- **emergent** — deliberately discover personality/history through play.

The player may mix levels by category.

Example: `I want to define family and upbringing, but leave personality and long-term goals emergent.`

### 7. Character relationships and home ties

Offer—not require—questions such as:
- Does the PC have living family or close friends?
- Do they have a home they care about?
- Is there a mentor, teacher, patron, rival, dependent, or obligation?
- Does the player want these relationships defined now, generated by the DM, or discovered later?

Do not turn every relationship into an adventure hook. Relationships may simply exist as normal parts of the character's life.

### 8. Starting motivation / opening situation

Ask how the player wants the campaign to begin:
- already pursuing a specific goal;
- ordinary life disrupted by events;
- looking for work/adventure;
- traveling;
- returning home;
- apprenticeship/training;
- serving an employer/patron;
- DM-generated opening;
- no strong preference.

The player controls the PC's decisions; this prompt only helps establish the first playable situation.

### 9. Known world vs. discovery

Ask how much setting information the player wants up front:
- broad player-facing overview;
- only what the PC would naturally know;
- very limited exposition, learn mostly through play;
- detailed primer if using an official setting.

Always preserve the difference between what the world contains and what the PC knows.

### 10. Prewritten modules and published adventures

Ask explicitly whether the player wants prewritten AD&D modules, Dungeon adventures, and other published scenarios to be part of the campaign.

Useful choices include:
- **actively include them** — regularly seed suitable published adventures into the world alongside original material;
- **use when they fit naturally** — published modules are first-class possibilities but are introduced only when they fit established geography, level/risk, chronology, and campaign causality;
- **mostly original** — prefer original adventures, using published material only occasionally;
- **inspiration only** — borrow ideas, monsters, locations, or structures without intentionally presenting full published scenarios;
- **no prewritten modules** — do not intentionally seed published adventures as playable scenarios;
- **no preference / surprise me**.

Optionally ask whether the player cares about a particular source family or era, such as classic TSR modules, Dungeon Magazine adventures, setting-specific modules, or any compatible AD&D 2e material. Do not require this level of detail.

This preference governs **opportunity frequency and use**, not a promise that a particular module will occur. Published adventures must still enter the campaign through normal world causality and may be missed, bypassed, altered, entered early or late, or never discovered.

Do not identify future modules, reveal titles merely because they are being considered, or spoil maps, keys, traps, NPC secrets, encounter locations, or other DM-only information.

A player may change this preference later. Apply the new preference prospectively: do not erase already established published locations, NPCs, or consequences merely because future module use is reduced or disabled.

### 11. Campaign scale and trajectory

Optionally ask whether the player has a preference for the campaign's likely scale:
- local and personal for a long time;
- gradually widening regional scope;
- eventually kingdoms/politics;
- eventually planes/cosmic material;
- no planned ceiling; let play decide.

This is not a promise of a predetermined story arc.

### 12. Content boundaries and emphasis

Offer the player an opportunity to identify material they want avoided, minimized, or emphasized.

Examples may include:
- graphic gore;
- horror;
- cruelty to children/animals;
- romance;
- sexual content;
- slavery;
- torture;
- intense interpersonal betrayal;
- bleakness;
- other personally unwanted content.

Do not require the player to justify a boundary.

### 13. Player authorship level

Ask how much direct world authorship the player wants outside their PC:
- **low** — player controls PC; DM creates world;
- **selective** — player may define chosen personal/background facts;
- **collaborative** — player enjoys helping define setting details;
- **ask each time** — DM offers occasional choices without assuming authorship preference.

Regardless of authorship level, the DM retains responsibility for hidden world state, opposition, consequences, secret information, and impartial adjudication.

## Minimal viable startup

A campaign may begin once the following are sufficiently established:
- a playable PC or enough mechanics to finish immediate character creation;
- a coherent starting location/situation;
- enough rules decisions to adjudicate likely opening play;
- any player-stated content boundaries that matter immediately.

Everything else may remain open.

Do not delay play merely to complete the questionnaire.

## Relationship to optional-rule review

Campaign startup discovery and optional-rule review are separate but adjacent processes. **The startup-discovery prompts do not replace or suppress the optional-rule review.**

Recommended order:
1. establish broad campaign/character premise;
2. run campaign-startup discovery prompts if the player wants them;
3. run common/useful optional-rule review under `OPTIONAL_RULES_STARTUP_POLICY.md`;
4. finish immediate character mechanics and opening-state generation;
5. begin play.

The DM may reorder items when a different sequence is more natural, but do not let either review become bureaucratic overhead.

## Canonicalization

Persist only startup answers that materially constrain campaign state or future generation.

Examples worth canonicalizing:
- official setting choice or deliberate wider-setting deferral;
- starting region/location facts;
- enduring tone/content-boundary preferences;
- character family/background facts;
- player authorship preference if it meaningfully affects future startup/world-generation behavior;
- prewritten-module/published-adventure preference;
- explicitly established starting motivation or obligation.

Do not checkpoint every casual aesthetic comment as immutable canon.

## Existing campaigns

This policy is primarily for new campaigns.

Do not automatically force an existing campaign through the full startup questionnaire.

If the player asks to review or revise campaign tone, setting scope, PC depth, authorship level, published-module preference, or related startup decisions later, use this policy as the question bank and apply changes prospectively where possible.

## Maintenance

The prompt list may evolve based on actual campaign-startup experience. Add only questions that repeatedly help players make useful choices; remove prompts that create more friction than value.

The goal is a fast, flexible conversation that helps the player decide how much they want to define—not a mandatory campaign-design worksheet.
