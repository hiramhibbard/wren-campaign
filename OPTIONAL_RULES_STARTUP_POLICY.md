# Wren Optional Rules Startup Policy — v1

Purpose: establish a small, useful, source-grounded set of AD&D 2nd Edition optional-rule decisions early in a campaign without forcing players to review every obscure option in the published corpus.

This policy governs **how optional rules are surfaced, deferred, activated, and changed**. It does not itself activate any optional rule.

Published source authority remains Hiram's uploaded AD&D 2e material. Campaign activation state remains canonical campaign state/rulings/checkpoints.

## Core principle

At the beginning of a **new campaign**, after the rules baseline is established but before ordinary gameplay materially depends on unresolved options, ask the player whether they want to review the common/useful optional-rule shortlist.

The player may:
- resolve all of them now;
- resolve only selected items;
- defer any or all of them;
- skip the review entirely and use core/default behavior until a choice is later made.

Do not require a decision merely because an option exists.

A deferred option is **inactive/unresolved**, not implicitly enabled.

## Later-session behavior

At the beginning of a later session, deferred common options may be reviewed **if the player chooses**. Do not repeatedly interrogate the player or block play because deferred options remain.

A concise reminder is allowed when useful, for example:

`You still have some optional rules deferred. We can review them now, or just play.`

Do not repeat this every session if the player has shown no interest. The player can always say `review optional rules`, name a specific rule, or otherwise request a change.

## Prospective activation / deactivation

Unless the player explicitly requests retroactive reconciliation and the DM determines it is safe and coherent, changing an optional rule applies **prospectively from the canonical decision point forward**.

Do not rewrite prior legitimate outcomes merely because a rule is later enabled or disabled.

If a change affects persistent character construction or advancement state, reconcile the smallest necessary canonical state and checkpoint the ruling before relying on it.

## Obscure or narrow optional rules

Do **not** burden startup with obscure, setting-specific, class-specific, item-specific, monster-specific, or rarely invoked optional rules.

Examples include individual supplement subsystems, horse personality tables, wand command words, race-book special variants, specialized psionic options, or setting-only procedures.

Such rules remain inactive unless:
- an active source/setting explicitly requires them;
- the player asks to use them;
- the campaign adopts the relevant subsystem/source scope explicitly.

The player may activate an otherwise omitted optional rule at any later time by clearly stating that they want it in use from then on. Before activation, retrieve the exact governing source, explain any material dependencies/conflicts, record the decision canonically, and apply it prospectively.

## Common/useful startup shortlist

The shortlist is intentionally compact. Present it in plain language with a one-line practical effect rather than reading rulebook text.

### 1. Weapon proficiencies

- source: PHB Chapter 5, Proficiencies (Optional)
- choice: `on / off`
- practical effect: characters track learned weapon use and suffer class-group nonproficiency penalties with unfamiliar weapons.
- note: PHB describes weapon proficiencies as tournament-level rules and optional in regular play.

### 2. Nonweapon proficiencies

- source: PHB Chapter 5, Proficiencies (Optional)
- choice: `on / off`
- practical effect: characters gain learned adventuring/background skills with source-defined proficiency checks.
- dependency: may be enabled independently of weapon proficiencies only if the campaign explicitly chooses that treatment; otherwise ask separately because the PHB distinguishes their optionality.

### 3. Fighter weapon specialization

- source: PHB Chapter 5, Weapon Specialization
- choice: `on / off`
- practical effect: eligible single-class fighters may spend additional weapon-proficiency slots for specialization benefits.
- dependency: meaningful only if the weapon-proficiency system is in use.

### 4. Encumbrance

- source: PHB Chapter 6, Encumbrance
- choice: `off / basic / specific`
- practical effect: carried weight can reduce movement and combat effectiveness.
- `basic`: category-based tournament rule.
- `specific`: finer-grained optional movement reductions.
- startup treatment: ask because encumbrance materially affects exploration/resource play throughout a campaign.

### 5. Spell components

- source: PHB Chapter 7, Spell Components (Optional Rule)
- choice: `on / off`
- practical effect: verbal/somatic/material requirements become mechanically binding; uncommon/valuable components may require actual acquisition.
- if off: use the core non-component casting assumptions from the governing source rather than selectively enforcing material components.

### 6. Maximum number of wizard spells per level

- source: PHB Chapter 1, Intelligence / Maximum Number of Spells per Level (Optional Rule)
- choice: `on / off`
- practical effect: Intelligence limits how many different spells a wizard may know in the spellbook at each spell level.
- note: this is distinct from daily memorization/casting capacity.

### 7. Initiative detail package

- sources: DMG Chapter 9, Table 41 Optional Modifiers to Initiative; Individual Initiative (Optional Rule); PHB spell casting-time references
- choices:
  - `standard group initiative only`
  - `group initiative + action-specific modifiers`
  - `individual initiative + action-specific modifiers`
- practical effect: determines whether weapon speed, spell casting time, creature size/natural attacks, and other action modifiers affect combat order, and whether each combatant rolls separately.
- caution: individual initiative is slower and the DMG specifically warns against it for large combats.

### 8. Weapon type vs. armor modifiers

- source: DMG Chapter 9, Weapon Type vs. Armor Modifiers (Optional Rule)
- choice: `on / off`
- practical effect: slashing/piercing/bludgeoning weapon effectiveness varies against armor types.
- note: useful for tactically detailed campaigns but adds lookup overhead.

### 9. Critical hits

- source: DMG Chapter 9, Critical Hits (Optional Rule)
- choice: `off / natural 20 double-damage dice / natural 20 immediate extra attack`
- practical effect: natural 20s gain an additional combat benefit under one of the DMG's suggested systems.
- symmetry: if active, apply the same governing rule to PCs and NPCs/monsters unless the source explicitly says otherwise.

### 10. Critical fumbles

- source: DMG Chapter 9, Critical Fumbles discussion
- choice: `on / off`
- practical effect: a natural 1 may create a non-damage mishap, normally costing the next round's attack/action recovery under the described system.
- note: keep separate from critical hits so a player can choose one without the other.

### 11. Full defensive parrying

- source: DMG Chapter 9, Parrying (Optional Rule)
- choice: `on / off`
- practical effect: a character can forfeit all actions for the round to concentrate entirely on defense and gain the source-defined AC benefit against applicable frontal attacks.

### 12. Hovering on Death's Door / negative hit points

- source: DMG Chapter 9, optional Hovering on Death's Door rule
- choice: `on / off`
- practical effect: characters may remain alive below 0 hp until the rule's negative-hp death threshold, with unconsciousness, bleeding, stabilization, healing, and recovery restrictions.
- importance: high-value startup decision because it materially changes lethality.

### 13. Training to gain levels

- source: DMG Chapter 8, Training (Optional Rule)
- choice: `on / off`
- practical effect: reaching the XP threshold does not immediately grant the level; qualifying instruction, time, cost, and checks may be required.
- importance: strongly affects campaign pacing, downtime, tutors, and economy.

### 14. Detailed jogging/running/chase rules

- source: PHB Chapter 14, Jogging and Running (Optional Rule)
- choice: `on / off`
- practical effect: uses exact speed multipliers plus Strength/Constitution checks and exhaustion instead of the simpler chase resolution.
- note: useful in movement/exploration-heavy campaigns; otherwise the simpler core chase procedure is adequate.

## Startup presentation

Do not dump all source mechanics at the player.

Preferred presentation:
1. ask whether they want to review common optional rules now;
2. if yes, present the shortlist in compact groups such as `character`, `combat`, `magic`, `survival/advancement`;
3. show current/default/deferred state when known;
4. explain only the options the player asks about in detail;
5. record resolved decisions canonically before relying on them.

For example, a compact first pass can name:
- proficiencies / specialization;
- encumbrance;
- spell components / wizard spell limit;
- initiative detail;
- armor-vs-weapon modifiers;
- critical hits/fumbles / parrying;
- death's door;
- level training;
- detailed running/chases.

## Default and unresolved semantics

- `active`: explicitly adopted by canonical campaign ruling/source requirement.
- `inactive`: explicitly rejected/disabled.
- `deferred`: player has postponed the decision; use core/default non-option behavior in the meantime.
- `unreviewed`: campaign has not yet addressed the option; use core/default non-option behavior.
- `scope-gated`: available only inside an explicitly active setting/class/item/supplement scope.

**Never infer `active` from source availability, compilation, prior familiarity, or the fact that a rule is common.**

## Conflict and dependency handling

Before changing an option:
1. retrieve the exact governing source if not already verified;
2. identify dependencies with existing character/campaign state;
3. detect conflicts with already active setting/supplement rules;
4. explain only material consequences to the player;
5. persist the explicit campaign decision.

If an option changes another option's meaning, resolve the dependency explicitly rather than silently selecting a compatible combination.

## Current Wren campaign boundary

This policy does not retroactively alter Wren's existing rules profile.

Existing verified campaign decisions remain authoritative, including the already-active Maximum Number of Spells per Level rule. Other options remain governed by current canonical rulings/checkpoints until Hiram chooses to review or change them.

## Maintenance of the shortlist

The shortlist may be revised when actual play demonstrates that:
- an omitted option is repeatedly useful across ordinary campaigns;
- a listed option is rarely relevant or creates disproportionate startup burden;
- a source clarification shows that an item was misclassified.

Changes to shortlist membership are infrastructure, not automatic campaign-rule changes.
