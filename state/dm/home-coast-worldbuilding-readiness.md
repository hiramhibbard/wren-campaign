# DM-ONLY Home Coast World-Building Readiness Audit

Status: current operational audit under `WORLD_BUILDER_RUNTIME_POLICY.md`.

This file records what the Home Coast **does and does not need generated yet**. It is not a declaration that unresolved things do not exist.

## Primary design mode

Use the **microscopic/local campaign area** approach for current Wren play.

Current established anchors and active-region runtime are sufficient to support ordinary play from Lowcove, with expansion outward as Wren's actions make details consequential.

Secondary approaches may trigger as needed:
- character-based for consequential NPC/mentor/family/background context;
- situation-based for the coastal magical phenomenon and any other established dominant process;
- sociological when settlement/culture/government questions become material;
- historical when clues/sites require minimum past truth;
- macroscopic only after a genuine larger-scale dependency.

## Current gap audit

### Exact published campaign setting
Status: **defer**.

Mystara is considered but not locked. No current action requires choosing the wider published setting now. Locking one solely to fill blank space would create unnecessary downstream geography/cosmology/politics.

Trigger to resolve: a player action, published scenario/source choice, wider-world travel, deity/cosmology/political fact, setting-specific monster/adventure, or other consequential dependency that cannot remain setting-neutral.

### Exact Home Coast political jurisdiction / kingdom
Status: **defer**.

Lowcove, Bridgeford, and Saltwick can function under current local runtime without establishing the name/form of the larger polity.

Trigger to resolve: authority, law, taxation, military responsibility, political travel, jurisdictional dispute, noble/official interaction, setting lock, or comparable consequence.

### Lowcove detailed settlement structure
Status: **minimum current canon sufficient**.

Known facts support home/family/village scenes: small predominantly human coastal community, fishing/local traffic, familiar to Wren.

Do not currently generate exact population, full street map, council/government, temple roster, trade statistics, every family, or every business.

Generate specific institutions/NPCs/services automatically when Wren seeks or interacts with them.

### Bridgeford details and route
Status: **defer until approach/travel/investigation**.

Only its name and role as a local inland market town are established. The explicit checkpoint 000005 boundary forbids inventing exact distance, road geometry, river crossings, jurisdiction, population, or relationship to the observatory without support.

On activation, use the Guidebook's local campaign area / towns procedures with current geographic constraints and normal travel/source rules.

### Saltwick broader settlement detail
Status: **minimum current canon sufficient**.

Established harbor role, Mrs. Tansy's boarding house, and Edric activity are enough until Wren returns or Saltwick produces off-screen consequences needing institutions/economy/authority.

On activation, use harbor/seafaring specialist routing plus Guidebook settlement/local-area procedures.

### Regional terrain/cartography beyond operational zones
Status: **defer**.

Temperate maritime/coastal climate and operational zones are sufficient. Do not generate full forty-mile local-area map, river network, road network, prevailing-wind map, or exact terrain mosaic until travel/exploration requires them.

### Population totals / settlement network
Status: **defer**.

The Guidebook provides population categories and settlement counts, but current play does not require selecting a Home Coast population class or inventing unvisited villages. Existing named settlements/labels remain as established/map-status facts only.

Trigger to resolve: economic/logistical question, political scale, travel network, military resources, market reach, demographic consequence, or source/adventure placement requiring it.

### Local monster populations / ecology
Status: **defer**.

No durable hostile population should be generated merely because Chapter Five includes Monsters and Ecology. Current encounter/ecology policy already requires source/ecological justification when a population becomes consequential.

Trigger to resolve: valid encounter table creation, evidence, active adventure/site, migration, rumor requiring truth, environmental pressure, or direct exploration.

### Additional adventure sites
Status: **no current quota/gap**.

Current play already contains meaningful unresolved adventure possibilities: the coastal worked-stone site/thread and the reported eastern-hills observatory, plus active hidden world processes.

Do not add a new dungeon/site merely because the Guidebook recommends sites of interest. `ADVENTURE_OPPORTUNITY_POLICY.md` will search published modules/Dungeon material automatically when a genuine world opportunity arises.

### History / mythology
Status: **progressive/deferred**.

Do not generate full Home Coast mythology or ancient chronology now. Resolve minimum history when the worked stones, observatory, mentor inquiry, outside seekers, settlement beliefs, or another consequential clue requires it.

### Current season / exact date
Status: **defer to chronology/rules dependency**.

The operational climate baseline is sufficient for current indoor/local play. Do not use World Builder climate tables to invent the season. Resolve only through established chronology/source/campaign decision when weather, daylight, agriculture, travel, or seasonal procedures require it.

## Current conclusion

No additional **campaign-world fact** is required right now merely to make the Home Coast playable.

The principal gap was procedural: there was no explicit source-backed automatic resolver for future unknown world detail. That gap is now handled by `WORLD_BUILDER_RUNTIME_POLICY.md` and `state/rulings/world-builder-triggers.md`.

When a current deferred item becomes consequential, resolve it automatically at the smallest useful scale rather than asking Hiram to request world-building work.
