# Home Coast Regional Runtime Profile

Status: canonical regional operating substrate for currently known/nearby Home Coast play.

This profile establishes only enough geography/environment/procedure routing to run weather, travel, encounters, and regional expansion consistently. It does not establish undiscovered sites, complete local history, monster populations, political geography, or the final published campaign setting.

## Known regional anchors

- **Lowcove** — Wren's small home coastal village/community.
- **Bridgeford** — inland market town known to Wren; approximately one day's travel on foot from Lowcove under the previously established travel guide.
- **Saltwick** — harbor settlement known to Wren by reputation and now part of the active campaign geography; approximately two days by road from Lowcove or about one long day by boat in good conditions under the established travel guide.
- **Sheltered shingle coves/coastal hollows** — representative local shore features; one such site is where Wren met Aldrin Hale.
- **Eastern hills / old observatory direction** — hills eastward; the reportedly half-ruined observatory lies roughly 10–12 days away on foot from Bridgeford according to Aldrin. Wren has not established the intervening terrain by personal travel.
- Other map labels such as Seabreeze Hamlet, Pine Hollow Farms, Looking Glass Cove, and Old Observatory remain valid map anchors, but detailed local state should be generated only as play makes them relevant.

## Climate baseline

The Home Coast has a **temperate maritime/coastal** operational climate baseline until a later setting lock establishes a more specific compatible climate.

Implications:
- meaningful seasonal variation exists, but the current exact season remains governed by chronology and should not be invented merely to fill this profile;
- wind is commonly relevant near shore and especially at sea;
- rain, drizzle, mist/fog, and changing cloud are plausible ordinary conditions;
- storms are meaningful hazards, particularly for small boats and exposed coastal travel;
- temperatures should normally remain within a temperate seasonal range unless a source, weather event, altitude, or magical phenomenon establishes otherwise.

If a later published-setting lock contradicts this baseline, reconcile the minimum affected climate assumptions explicitly rather than silently rewriting past observed weather.

## Runtime zones

These are operational zones, not exact cartographic boundaries.

### Lowcove village and immediate settled shore

Character: small, predominantly human, inhabited coastal community with ordinary fishing/local traffic and familiar surroundings for Wren.

Procedure routing:
- ordinary village/interior activity: no routine generic monster-style random encounter check merely because time passes;
- encounters arise from keyed events, NPC/world-state activity, social traffic, accidents, weather consequences, or rare threats justified by current state;
- weather is tracked when it changes scene conditions, fishing/boat use, travel, visibility, or scheduled world processes;
- navigation/getting-lost is normally irrelevant for Wren in familiar immediate surroundings unless exceptional conditions change that;
- world-state changes can raise risk dynamically; "safe" is not permanent plot protection.

Encounter ingredients when needed:
- villagers/fishers/family/local workers;
- small boats and ordinary coastal activity;
- traders/visitors whose presence is plausible from established routes;
- mundane wildlife/animals;
- weather/environmental incidents;
- rumors/reports and world-actor consequences;
- rare danger only when justified by established or newly resolved world state.

### Lowcove–Bridgeford corridor / settled inland route

Character: known connection between the coast and Bridgeford; route/farmland detail remains deliberately incomplete.

Procedure routing:
- meaningful day travel activates travel/readiness/time procedures;
- use the closest applicable governing DMG wilderness terrain entry only after actual terrain for the segment is known or can be reasonably classified; do not force the whole route into one terrain type;
- settled/patrolled population modifiers require encounter tables reflecting settled traffic before use;
- weather can affect visibility, pace, comfort, route conditions, and encounter context;
- getting-lost is normally less relevant on a known maintained route, but poor visibility, detours, damaged routes, or leaving the route can activate it.

Encounter ingredients:
- farmers/laborers/local travelers;
- merchants, carts, livestock, messengers, or officials as locally plausible;
- ordinary animals/wildlife;
- roadside/weather incidents;
- dynamically active threats/factions only when world state establishes them.

### Bridgeford and environs

Character: inland market-town environment; details should expand when Wren next interacts there.

Procedure routing:
- ordinary town scenes use social/keyed/world-state encounters rather than wilderness monster checks;
- roads/outskirts may use the settled-route encounter procedure when meaningful travel occurs;
- market, authority, economic, rumor, faction, and visitor activity should be created as the town enters the active horizon rather than prewritten exhaustively.

### Saltwick harbor and near approaches

Character: harbor settlement and coastal traffic node.

Procedure routing:
- ordinary settlement scenes use social/keyed/world-state encounters;
- harbor waters and approach travel activate boat/sea weather and water movement rules when consequential;
- local marine traffic, docks, ships, travelers, trade, weather, and faction/world-actor effects can generate encounters without implying combat;
- exact harbor institutions/economy/politics expand only as needed.

### Remote coast / coves / exposed shore

Character: less-settled shoreline beyond routine village activity.

Procedure routing:
- weather active during meaningful exposure/travel;
- navigation/visibility/tide/shore conditions may matter depending on route and current facts;
- random encounter procedure may become active during meaningful exploration using the closest applicable DMG terrain classification and an actual local encounter table/ingredients profile;
- resource/readiness becomes more relevant as distance from settlement/resupply increases.

Encounter ingredients should be derived from currently established local ecology, human traffic, active factions/forces, weather, coastal phenomenon effects, and source/adventure material rather than a permanent generic monster list.

### Coastal waters / open sea

Procedure routing:
- use core DMG water movement and ship-weather rules when applicable;
- daily sea weather can be generated from the verified ship-weather projection when weather matters to movement/safety;
- DMG Ocean wilderness encounter checks apply during qualifying wilderness sea travel unless a more specific active source/profile governs;
- encounter content must reflect current marine traffic/ecology/world actors; Ocean check frequency does not itself imply monsters;
- small-boat capability, supplies, wind/weather, navigation, visibility, and elapsed time are material readiness dependencies.

### Eastern hills / observatory approaches

Character: currently only broadly known/reported to Wren; not personally traversed.

Procedure routing once Wren approaches/enters:
- activate hills/wilderness travel profile;
- use DMG Hills encounter check projection unless a later specific governing source/profile supersedes it;
- navigation/getting-lost, terrain movement, supplies/readiness, weather, camps/watches, and encounter checks become active as applicable;
- create local encounter ingredients and active populations/factions before the first check that could require them;
- do not establish the full intervening geography merely because the observatory is known to exist.

## Encounter profile generation

A zone may carry encounter ingredients without a finalized roll table.

Before the first random encounter check in a zone that lacks a prepared table:
1. load current zone facts and active DM world state;
2. identify ordinary social traffic, wildlife/ecology, active factions/populations, weather/environmental possibilities, and source/adventure entries actually relevant there;
3. construct a local table using an appropriate distribution before rolling the encounter result;
4. preserve special/one-use results and current dynamic weights as DM state if repeated checks are likely;
5. do not add unrelated monsters simply to guarantee combat.

## Lore substrate

Wren's upbringing makes ordinary local coastal life, fishing/seamanship, basic routes to familiar nearby settlements, and common local practical knowledge plausible without repeated lore checks.

Deeper histories, religions, authorities, feuds, old events, monster populations, ruins, supernatural explanations, and wider political context remain unestablished until source/play/hidden causality requires them.

Local beliefs and folklore may be accurate, partially accurate, distorted, mistaken, intentionally false, or unresolved. Use `state/rulings/knowledge-reliability-and-rumors.md` rather than promoting local belief directly into truth.

## Dynamic dependencies

Refresh only affected regional runtime state when world state changes.

Examples:
- a new patrol/trade route -> settled encounter ingredients and traffic frequency;
- predator/monster/faction migration -> affected zone encounter eligibility/weights;
- storm/fog -> sea/visibility/navigation/encounter context;
- public supernatural incident -> rumor pool/community pressure;
- disrupted road/bridge -> route, travel, traffic, supplies, encounter profile.

The base regional profile remains stable unless underlying geography/climate/settlement facts change.
