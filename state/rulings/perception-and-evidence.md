# Perception, Investigation, and Evidence Procedure

This procedure governs how the DM presents sensory information, investigation results, clues, and inferred meaning during play.

Exact AD&D 2e mechanics for vision, hearing, searching, secret doors, traps, proficiencies, surprise, light, invisibility, detection, and similar procedures remain source-governed.

## Trigger
Use this procedure when:
- Wren examines, searches, listens, tracks, studies, compares, scouts, maps, questions, or otherwise investigates;
- a hidden feature could be perceived through ordinary senses or established expertise;
- a creature/faction/environmental process should have left physical or social evidence;
- the distinction between observation and interpretation matters to player choice.

## Observation first
Present the best-supported observable facts before hidden explanation.

Observable channels may include:
- sight: shape, color, condition, wear, residue, arrangement, movement, tracks, damage, craftsmanship;
- sound: volume, direction, repetition, rhythm, distance estimate, material qualities;
- smell/taste: smoke, rot, salt, blood, oil, herbs, damp, animal musk, food, chemical or magical clues only when legitimately perceptible;
- touch: temperature, texture, moisture, vibration, looseness, weight/resistance;
- ordinary social evidence: behavior, clothing, accent, tools, cargo, traffic, prices, absences, repairs, rumors.

Do not label hidden cause as an observed fact.

## Interpretation
An interpretation may be supplied automatically only when one or more are true:
- the conclusion is obvious to an ordinary competent person in Wren's situation;
- Wren has established training, profession, proficiency, spell effect, or personal experience that supports it;
- a governing rule/check grants the inference;
- previously established facts make the conclusion effectively certain.

Otherwise present the evidence and let Hiram infer.

When useful, phrase supported-but-uncertain inference as degree of confidence rather than objective truth.

## Investigation method
If Hiram's declared method is sufficient to resolve the relevant distinction, adjudicate it without demanding unnecessary micro-description.

Ask for method/position/tool clarification only when materially different methods would change:
- what can be found;
- risk/exposure;
- elapsed time;
- noise;
- resource use;
- whether a rule/check applies.

Do not require pixel-hunting for obvious competent substeps. Do require specificity where choosing the method is itself the challenge.

## Hidden checks
When the governing rules require a secret check, preserve uncertainty. Do not reveal failure by saying "you find nothing because you failed." Present only what Wren experiences.

A failed hidden check does not necessarily imply no evidence exists; it means Wren did not obtain the unavailable result through that attempt.

## Evidence generation
When an established creature, actor, population, weather event, occupation, trade pattern, hazard, or magical process has been present long enough to leave traces, determine whether evidence should exist from its actual behavior and environment.

Evidence may include tracks, spoor, nests, discarded food, prey remains, wear, smoke, camps, tools, graffiti, repairs, barricades, altered vegetation, missing animals, changed traffic, residue, smell, sound, witnesses, prices, shortages, or local behavior.

Do not fabricate evidence solely to steer Wren toward a prepared scene. Evidence should arise from established or newly resolved causal state.

## Signal-to-noise discipline
Not every sensory detail is important. Include ordinary, harmless, mistaken, irrelevant, and ambiguous details where natural so that description does not function as a meta-game highlighter.

Do not bury every clue in noise either. If a clue is plainly visible to Wren, describe it plainly.

## Knowledge boundaries
Separate:
- observed fact;
- Wren's supported inference;
- NPC report/rumor;
- Established DM Truth.

Use `state/rulings/knowledge-reliability-and-rumors.md` for testimony/belief reliability.

Do not convert evocative narration into hidden truth accidentally during checkpointing. Persist consequential evidence and what Wren learned at the epistemic level actually established.
