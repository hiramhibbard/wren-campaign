# NPC Generation and Portrayal Protocol

This file defines how original NPCs are prepared and how established NPCs are portrayed. Exact AD&D 2e rules, class restrictions, ability-score meanings, alignment effects, reaction/morale procedures, and published NPC statistics remain governed by Hiram's uploaded sources.

## Core generation order

For an original NPC, determine durable details in this order unless a published source already establishes them:

1. **Published/source facts first.** If the NPC comes from a published adventure, setting, monster entry, article, or other uploaded source, preserve the published race/species, class, level, alignment, statistics, abilities, personality, motives, equipment, relationships, and other substantive facts. Fill only genuine gaps and keep additions consistent with the source.
2. **Established world context.** Determine why this person exists here: location, culture, social position, faction/religion, occupation, age/life stage, local economy, education opportunities, and current purpose.
3. **Role requirements.** Identify capabilities the role actually requires. A professional cartographer, experienced ship captain, sage, spellcasting mentor, soldier, priest, thief, craftsperson, official, or adventurer should possess the knowledge, training, class/level, proficiencies, ability profile, and resources needed to function plausibly in that role.
4. **Mechanical profile.** Determine only the statistics currently needed. Use exact ability scores/class/level/combat values when mechanics are likely to matter; otherwise a qualitative profile may remain sufficient until promoted.
5. **Alignment / ethos.** Determine alignment from source canon, class/religious restrictions, established behavior, culture/faction, values, and personal history. Alignment informs beliefs, priorities, methods, relationships, and likely choices, but does not replace personality.
6. **Personality and history synthesis.** Establish motives, values, fears, ambitions, habits, social style, prejudices/biases, loyalties, relationships, and enough background to explain the NPC's present role without inventing unnecessary biography.
7. **Speech and cognition.** Let Intelligence, Wisdom, Charisma, education, occupation, culture, languages, literacy, age, emotional state, and personality shape comprehension and dialogue.
8. **Constrained variation/randomness.** Use genuine random generation where useful to prevent repetitive NPCs, but random results operate inside the plausible space established above. Randomness may select among plausible traits, scores, quirks, secondary skills, appearance details, or history elements; it does not override required capabilities or established world logic.
9. **Consistency pass.** Before the NPC's hidden profile becomes consequential, check whether role, ability scores, alignment, education, skills, personality, social status, resources, history, and current behavior can plausibly coexist. Resolve contradictions before presenting consequences.
10. **Canonicalization.** Once a hidden statistic, alignment, motive, personality fact, skill, relationship, or other trait has materially informed play, persist it at the appropriate NPC significance tier. Do not silently regenerate it later for convenience.

## Context constrains probability; it does not dictate destiny

Occupation, wealth, social class, race/species, culture, or living conditions should affect what backgrounds, training, literacy, resources, and opportunities are plausible, but they do not rigidly determine innate ability or alignment.

Examples:
- A person living in poverty can be exceptionally intelligent; limited schooling may instead affect literacy, vocabulary, contacts, formal knowledge, and opportunity.
- A low-Intelligence person can have excellent practical judgment, deep experience, or narrow professional competence, especially with strong Wisdom and long practice.
- A scholarly or magical mentor who teaches difficult theory should possess the intelligence, knowledge, training, and mechanical qualifications that role requires.
- A highly intelligent person may still be foolish, impulsive, cowardly, cruel, socially awkward, poorly educated, or ignorant of a subject outside their experience.
- A laborer, noble, priest, criminal, merchant, soldier, or beggar can belong to any alignment unless setting/source/class/religious constraints or established behavior narrow the possibilities.

Unusual combinations are welcome when they produce a coherent person. When an unlikely result survives the consistency pass, preserve the reason it makes sense rather than normalizing everyone toward stereotypes.

## Alignment

Alignment is a roleplaying/ethos guide and a mechanical fact where AD&D rules care about it. It is not a complete personality description.

For significant NPCs, alignment should inform:
- moral priorities and limits;
- attitudes toward law, authority, obligation, tradition, individual freedom, community, mercy, exploitation, loyalty, and self-interest;
- choices under pressure;
- willingness to cooperate, deceive, betray, sacrifice, forgive, punish, or exploit when those choices intersect the NPC's values;
- compatibility/conflict with religions, factions, classes, magic items, henchmen, employers, and companions where the rules/world make alignment relevant.

Alignment should **not** determine:
- Intelligence, education, competence, friendliness, humor, courage, generosity in every circumstance, profession, social class, or exact personality;
- a single automatic response to every situation;
- information the NPC could not know.

Two NPCs of the same alignment may have radically different personalities, goals, methods, social styles, and relationships.

### How alignment is chosen

Use this priority:
1. published/source alignment;
2. class, deity/religion, monster, faction, magic-item, or setting restrictions;
3. already established actions and values;
4. culture/social context and personal history as soft influences;
5. deliberate selection or weighted random choice among the remaining plausible alignments when several fit equally well.

Do not assign alignment by a flat universal random roll unless a governing source specifically calls for it. Do not make every profession or social group morally uniform.

### Alignment secrecy and discovery

An NPC's exact alignment is DM-only unless Wren has legitimately learned it through established magic, class ability, trustworthy revelation, or other in-world evidence. NPCs should normally be presented through words, deeds, loyalties, beliefs, and patterns of behavior rather than explicit alignment labels.

Treat alignment as capable of gradual change when sustained actions and developed beliefs justify it. Do not flip alignment because of one minor inconsistency; likewise do not preserve a label indefinitely when the NPC's established conduct has genuinely shifted.

## Ability scores and portrayal

When exact scores are established, use the AD&D source descriptions as anchors.

- **Intelligence:** memory, reasoning, learning, abstraction, pattern recognition, vocabulary range, and complexity of explanation. Low Intelligence may mean slower comprehension or simpler reasoning; high Intelligence may mean faster learning and more sophisticated problem-solving.
- **Wisdom:** judgment, common sense, intuition, practical understanding, perceptiveness, self-control, and awareness of consequences.
- **Charisma:** force of personality, presence, leadership, persuasion, magnetism, or intimidation.

These interact rather than collapse into one 'smart/dumb' axis. A brilliant but low-Wisdom NPC may reason elegantly toward terrible decisions. A modest-Intelligence, high-Wisdom veteran may be poor at theory but excellent at reading danger and people.

## Skills, jobs, and competence

Professional competence should be grounded in class, level, proficiencies, ability scores, training, tools, experience, and setting opportunity as appropriate.

- Do not give an NPC a prestigious or technically demanding occupation and then assign incompatible capabilities without an established explanation.
- Do not assume every capable professional is an adventuring-class character; ordinary NPC competence may be represented qualitatively or through relevant proficiencies/DM adjudication until exact stats matter.
- If an NPC attempts something outside established competence and the outcome is uncertain, use the appropriate AD&D procedure rather than retroactively giving the NPC whatever skill would guarantee success.

## Personality generation

Personality should be partly constrained by alignment/history and partly independent so NPCs remain varied.

Useful dimensions include:
- ambition/contentment;
- courage/risk tolerance;
- patience/temper;
- trust/suspicion;
- generosity/possessiveness;
- optimism/pessimism;
- formality/familiarity;
- honesty/deviousness within alignment limits;
- sociability/reserve;
- humor style;
- pride/shame;
- empathy/callousness;
- curiosity/conservatism;
- religious or cultural commitment;
- attachment to family, place, status, craft, cause, or wealth.

Random selection can be used among plausible dimensions, followed by a consistency pass. Avoid repeatedly generating the same articulate, competent, mildly sarcastic modern personality.

## When exact stats are created

- **Walk-on:** usually no exact stat block unless a roll/combat/procedure requires it.
- **Recurring NPC:** establish qualitative ability/personality/knowledge first; exact stats may remain unset.
- **Mechanically relevant NPC:** establish enough exact stats before the relevant uncertainty is resolved.
- **Henchman/companion/major NPC:** maintain a durable mechanical and relationship record suitable for repeated adjudication.

Stats that become necessary during play should be determined before the roll/outcome they govern, using the context-first process above, and then persisted if durable.

## Randomness and fairness

When randomness is used for hidden NPC generation:
- use genuine random rolls rather than choosing a preferred result and calling it random;
- define the plausible range/table/weights first from source and context;
- roll once and accept the result if it passes established constraints;
- if a result violates a hard source/role constraint, treat it as an invalid generation result and use the predefined regeneration/selection rule rather than changing it after seeing narrative consequences;
- preserve unusual valid results;
- once consequential, do not reroll for convenience.

The purpose of constrained randomness is variety and impartiality, not incoherence.

## Portrayal during play

Before speaking/acting as a significant NPC, ground the response in:
- established alignment/ethos;
- personality and motives;
- Intelligence/Wisdom/Charisma or qualitative cognitive profile;
- education, occupation, culture, rank, languages and literacy;
- current emotional state and relationship to Wren;
- actual knowledge, beliefs, rumors, suspicions, secrets, and blind spots;
- current risks, incentives, obligations, faction orders, and scheduled plans.

NPC actions should emerge from that combined state. Dice resolve genuine uncertainty where the AD&D rules call for them; dice do not substitute for established character.
