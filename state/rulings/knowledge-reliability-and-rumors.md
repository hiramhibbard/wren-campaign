# Knowledge Reliability, Rumor, Error, and Deception Procedure — v1

This procedure determines what NPCs/reporters believe, how accurate their information is, and when rumor/error/deception should be fixed by context, randomization, or deferred truth assignment.

It does not make NPCs omniscient and does not make every rumor secretly meaningful.

## Trigger

Use this procedure when a consequential claim, report, rumor, memory, interpretation, accusation, folk belief, scholarly conclusion, professional judgment, or deliberate statement has an unresolved relationship to underlying truth.

Do not invoke it when source/canonical truth already fixes both what the speaker knows and whether the statement is accurate.

## Step 1 — establish information access

Determine what basis the speaker/source actually has:
- firsthand observation;
- repeated firsthand experience;
- professional expertise;
- trusted secondhand report;
- ordinary hearsay;
- folk tradition;
- written/scholarly source;
- faction/official intelligence;
- inference from evidence;
- magical/supernatural information;
- memory of an older event;
- no meaningful basis.

Access constrains what the speaker can plausibly know. Intelligence does not create information they never received.

## Step 2 — establish contextual reliability pressures

Consider only relevant established factors, such as:
- professional competence/expertise;
- Intelligence, Wisdom, perception, memory, education, literacy, and language ability;
- distance, darkness, fog, noise, fear, intoxication, fatigue, injury, haste, or emotional stress;
- age of the information and opportunities for conditions to have changed;
- local cultural assumptions, superstition, prejudice, propaganda, or conventional explanations;
- personal/faction bias;
- incentives to exaggerate, simplify, conceal, protect, impress, frighten, recruit, profit, save face, or deceive;
- whether the claim is inside or outside the speaker's actual domain of competence.

Do not equate sincerity with accuracy.

## Step 3 — separate belief from intent

Track two distinct questions:

1. **What does the speaker believe?**
2. **What are they trying to communicate?**

Possible states include:
- believes true and reports sincerely;
- believes partly true and reports sincerely;
- sincerely mistaken;
- uncertain but presents uncertainty honestly;
- uncertain but overstates confidence;
- knows truth but omits material information;
- knowingly distorts;
- knowingly lies/fabricates;
- repeats a rumor without endorsing it.

A dishonest NPC may accidentally say something true. A truthful NPC may confidently be wrong.

## Step 4 — determine truth relation

If established source/canonical facts already decide the claim, use them.

If several truth relations remain genuinely plausible, define a context-weighted hidden outcome space before rolling. Typical outcome classes are:
- substantially accurate;
- accurate observation with wrong explanation;
- partial truth with missing/distorted detail;
- outdated but formerly accurate;
- distorted secondhand/folk tradition;
- completely mistaken;
- deliberate fabrication/manipulation.

Weights are not universal. A veteran pilot describing local currents should normally be weighted very differently from a frightened witness describing distant lights through fog.

Use genuine secret randomness when multiple plausible outcomes remain and no established fact determines the result. Roll once, accept a valid result, and preserve it once consequential.

## Deferred truth assignment

The underlying truth does not always need to be generated immediately.

If a rumor can exist without any current clue, action, clock, or causal dependency requiring its objective truth, it may remain:

`rumor exists -> speaker/source state established -> truth status unresolved`

Before a consequential clue, hidden action, encounter, or resolution depends on the answer, establish the minimum necessary underlying truth and persist it before presenting the dependent consequence.

Do not resolve every tavern rumor merely because it was spoken.

## Durable claim record

For a consequential information item, preserve as applicable:
- claim/content;
- speaker/source;
- source basis/access;
- speaker belief state;
- communicative intent;
- confidence level if relevant;
- objective truth relation, if established;
- underlying Established DM Truth reference, if any;
- distortion/error origin;
- who knows/heard/believes it;
- contradictions/retractions;
- whether Wren treats it as fact, rumor, suspicion, or inference;
- resolution status.

Do not expose hidden reliability/truth metadata to Wren unless he legitimately discovers it.

## NPC relationship beliefs

Use the same procedure for social judgments when uncertainty matters: whether an NPC likes/trusts Wren, misreads his motives, believes gossip about him, remembers him incorrectly, or forms an inaccurate first impression.

Established personality, alignment/ethos, prior treatment, Charisma/reaction procedures, motives, culture, and circumstances constrain the plausible response. Genuine random reaction or hidden variation may settle unresolved attitude within that space.

Once consequential, preserve the resulting attitude/belief and its causes rather than rerolling for convenience.

## Rumor ecology

Rumors may change as they spread. When propagation itself matters, transformations must arise from identifiable transmission conditions such as:
- retelling through multiple people;
- translation/language mismatch;
- local bias or superstition;
- deliberate propaganda;
- compression of a complicated event;
- conflation with older folklore;
- elapsed time;
- new evidence.

Do not mutate a rumor arbitrarily between scenes. Record durable transformed variants when they become consequential.

## Fairness rules

- Do not make information false merely because Wren trusted it.
- Do not make it true merely because Wren distrusted it.
- Do not retroactively turn an NPC into a liar to preserve a mystery.
- Do not make every strange rumor point to the main arc.
- Some oddities and rumors must remain mundane, mistaken, unrelated, or unresolved when that is what established context/random resolution produces.
- Established DM Truth wins over later convenience; random truth/error results cannot overwrite established causality.

## Checkpointing

Checkpoint consequential new beliefs, lies, rumors, resolved truth relations, misinformation propagated to important actors, and changes to Wren's knowledge under the appropriate knowledge/DM-state domains. Preserve epistemic categories exactly.
