# Creature Ecology, Behavior, and Encounter Embodiment Procedure

This procedure turns active AD&D 2e monster source material into coherent world behavior rather than treating creatures as combat stat blocks.

The governing AD&D 2e monster source remains authoritative. Uploaded supplements remain inactive unless explicitly active for the relevant scope.

## Trigger
Use when a creature or creature population becomes consequential through encounter, lair, region activation, evidence, migration, faction/ecology interaction, treasure, or repeated presence.

## Source fields
As applicable, retrieve/use:
- climate/terrain;
- frequency;
- organization;
- activity cycle;
- diet;
- Intelligence;
- treasure;
- alignment;
- number appearing;
- morale;
- Combat;
- Habitat/Society;
- Ecology;
- active setting/adventure-specific overrides.

Do not retrieve every field when only one narrow mechanical value is needed.

## Embodiment questions
Resolve only consequential questions:
- why is the creature here;
- what is it currently doing;
- what does it need or seek;
- where does it rest/lair;
- when is it active;
- whether it is solitary, social, territorial, migratory, domesticated, summoned, controlled, trapped, or displaced;
- what signs its presence leaves;
- what it hunts/eats/avoids/fears;
- what predators, prey, rivals, allies, servants, leaders, young, dependents, or neighbors matter;
- what causes it to fight, flee, stalk, bargain, surrender, ignore, defend, or pursue;
- why it has treasure and whether it carries, uses, hoards, guards, trades, inherited, or merely occupies it.

## Intent before initiative
Before hostilities, apply established motive/orders/behavior first. If response remains uncertain, route to the AD&D 2e reaction procedure.

Once combat or danger is underway, use source morale and current circumstances when checks are called for. Do not force creatures to fight to the death unless source/established motive/morale supports it.

Low or animal Intelligence does not mean suicidal stupidity. It limits reasoning, planning, abstraction, communication, and adaptation according to the source; instinctive behavior may still be effective.

## Evidence footprint
Established creatures/populations should generate plausible evidence from their actual behavior and environment when enough time/opportunity exists.

Use `state/rulings/perception-and-evidence.md` to determine what Wren can perceive and infer.

Evidence should be causal, not a breadcrumb guarantee. Some creatures are stealthy, incorporeal, magical, clean, transient, or otherwise leave little/no ordinary trace.

## Population and regional effects
If a creature becomes a durable population rather than a one-off encounter, promote it under `REGIONAL_RUNTIME_POLICY.md` / `REGIONAL_RUNTIME_TEMPLATES.md` when it can alter ecology, travel safety, settlement behavior, encounter content, rumors, trade, prey/predators, or faction state.

A random encounter result may establish an individual or transient group without establishing a breeding/local population unless source/circumstances support that inference.

## Lairs and sites
When creatures occupy an active bounded site, link their behavior to `SITE_RUNTIME_POLICY.md`: territories, patrols, alarms, rest areas, food/water, neighbors, defenses, escape routes, treasure, and response to intrusion.

Do not force every source monster into a mundane ecological model. Undead, constructs, extraplanar beings, summoned creatures, magically sustained entities, and unique monsters may have explicitly non-natural ecology.

## Treasure
Use treasure fields as source inputs, not automatic piles appearing beside monsters. Determine placement/provenance when consequential. A carried patrol, a lair hoard, tribute, stolen goods, grave goods, ritual objects, and an inherited magic item are different world facts.

## Persistence
Once creature behavior, population state, lair relationship, evidence, motive, or treasure provenance materially affects play, preserve it at the appropriate player-facing/DM-only level rather than rerolling or reinventing it later.
